## What Circular Express Box is

Circular Express Box is a recycling program in a box. A customer buys a box, fills it with one clean stream of recyclables, and ships it back with the prepaid FedEx label that comes with every box. The material goes to a regional recycling hub, gets processed into sustainable raw material, and re-enters the world as real products through the Geared for GREEN family (apparel, furniture, building products). Every shipment is weighed and tracked, so customers see exactly what they diverted, and every shipment earns rewards points.
Because collection rides on FedEx instead of trucks and routes, CEB works anywhere in the United States, for a single household or a hundred locations, with zero new infrastructure.

## Who uses CEB, and how

### Individual consumers

People who want their recycling to actually get recycled, with proof. They typically buy a **Single Box ($99)**, keep it in the kitchen, garage, or home office, and fill it over weeks. When it is full, they seal it, print their prepaid FedEx label from their account, and drop the box at any FedEx location. Their dashboard shows pounds diverted, and points accumulate toward discounts on gear made from recycled materials. Then they order the next box.

### Businesses and facilities: gyms, studios, offices, cafes, campuses

The core customer. A facility places boxes where the waste actually happens: next to the water-bottle bins at a gym, in the break room, behind the coffee bar. Members and staff toss recyclables in all week; there is no sorting job because each box collects one labeled stream. When a box fills, whoever runs the program seals it and hands it to the FedEx driver who already comes to the building, no new pickups, no hauler contract.
Most start with the **3-Box Pack ($269)**, one box per high-traffic spot, and step up to the **10-Box Pack ($749)** for campuses and multi-room deployments. The payoff beyond the recycling itself: the dashboard turns their shipments into member-facing impact numbers ("our gym kept 118 lbs out of the landfill this season") and ESG-ready reporting, and teams can pool reward points to outfit staff in recycled-bottle gear.

### Brands and organizations: co-branded take-back programs

The partnership tier, and the reason for the Warner Music conversation. Every physical box has two built-in slots: a commodity sticker (what stream this box collects) and a "Supported by" sponsor panel (whose program this is). That means a brand can launch a **national take-back program without building anything**: CEB prints boxes carrying the brand's logo and a custom material stream, say, vinyl records, places them in stores, at events, or ships them to fans, and FedEx brings every box back. CEB handles processing and delivers campaign-level impact reporting the brand can use for ESG and PR.
Community programs work the same way: for example, boxes go to Miami Waterkeeper beach cleanups, volunteers fill them on the sand, and the season rolls up into a sustainability report. Vinyl records are the newest custom stream, currently piloting with the music industry.

## How the box works, start to finish

  - **Order.** Choose Single, 3-Pack, or 10-Pack on the website. Boxes ship flat to your door and set up in about a minute. Every box includes liners, its material sticker, and a prepaid FedEx return label tied to your account.
  - **Fill.** Toss in the accepted materials for that box's stream as you go. One box, one stream, that is what keeps the material clean enough to be worth recycling.
  - **Ship.** When it is full: seal it, log in and print the prepaid label, and either drop it at any FedEx location or hand it to the FedEx driver already visiting your building. Shipping costs you nothing extra; it is included in the box price.
  - **Processing.** The box arrives at a regional recycling hub, where the material is weighed, recorded to your account, and processed into sustainable raw material.
  - **Rebirth.** That material feeds real product lines: recycled-fabric apparel (6 bottles make 1 hat, 10 make a shirt), furniture, benches, building sheets, trash bags. This is the closed loop, and it is verifiable, Geared for GREEN has recycled 1.7B+ lbs since 1985 and put 950M+ lbs back into products.
  - **Credit.** The moment your shipment is received, your dashboard updates (pounds diverted, CO2e avoided) and points land in your account. Then the cycle repeats with your next box.

## What goes in

  | Standard streams | Custom streams (partner programs) | Never |
|---|---|---|
  | Plastic bottles & containers, Aluminum cans, Paper & cardboard, *Other recyclables on request* | Vinyl records (new), Event & venue waste, Textiles, *Nearly any clean stream, built to order* | Food waste & liquids, Glass, Batteries & electronics, Hazardous or medical waste |

The one-stream-per-box rule is not bureaucracy, it is the business model: uncontaminated single-material loads are what recyclers can actually turn into new products at quality. The sticker system makes the rule effortless for the person tossing items in.

## How rewards work

Every received shipment earns points. Points redeem for discounts on sustainable products made from the same kinds of materials customers ship, for example 400 points takes 20% off the 6-Bottle GREEN Hat, 350 points takes $10 off the Peaks Polo. Deliberately, rewards are not inflated "cash back" promises; they close the loop personally, you shipped the bottles, you wear the hat. Organizations can pool points to reward staff, members, students, or volunteers.

## How tracking and reporting work

Every box is tied to an account, and every shipment is weighed on receipt. From that single stream of data:

  - **Individuals** see lifetime pounds diverted and points earned.
  - **Facilities** see per-location, per-season totals they can put in front of members and in ESG reports: boxes shipped, pounds by material, CO2e avoided, and what the material became (so many hats, benches, sheets).
  - **Brand programs** get campaign-level rollups across every participating location, packaged as a sustainability report they can publish, the same report format used for the Miami Waterkeeper cleanup season shown on the website.
