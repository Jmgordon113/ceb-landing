# The CEB Website: Complete Overview & How It Works

*Circular Express Box · A Geared for GREEN company · August 2026*

**Live preview:** [jmgordon113.github.io/ceb-landing](https://jmgordon113.github.io/ceb-landing/)  ·  **Code:** [github.com/Jmgordon113/ceb-landing](https://github.com/Jmgordon113/ceb-landing)

## What this site is

The site is a single-page marketing landing page for Circular Express Box, the "recycling program in a box" from Geared for GREEN. It replaces the previous WordPress homepage, which suffered from an AI-generated hero image with garbled text, an illegible logo, placeholder testimonials, typos, and a developer's name in the footer.
The page has one job: make CEB look like the real, credible, national recycling company it is, and move two audiences to act. Consumers and small teams should click *Get Your Box*; brands and organizations (like Warner Music) should see themselves in the *For Brands* section and reach out.
The design was built from a competitive teardown of Trashie, TerraCycle, and Ridwell. We borrowed what works (effort-anchored copy, visible pricing, transparency about where materials go, real photography) and avoided what hurts them (audience overload, hidden prices, template aesthetics, overpromised rewards).

## 1. Announcement bar

The thin dark-green strip at the very top.

**What it shows:**
"Now accepting vinyl records • Nationwide shipping via FedEx."

**What it does:**
Plants the two newest, most differentiating facts before a visitor reads anything else. The vinyl mention is deliberate: anyone arriving from the music industry sees their use case in the first second.

**How it works:**
Static text, easily swapped for any current campaign. One line in the HTML.

## 2. Navigation

**What it shows:**
The round CEB badge logo at proper size, the wordmark, six section links (How It Works, What Goes In, Rewards, Pricing, For Brands, FAQ), a Log In link, and a green *Get Your Box* button.

**What it does:**
Keeps the single most valuable action, getting a box, visible at all times. Log In preserves access for existing customers who print shipping labels from their accounts.

**How it works:**
The bar sticks to the top and gains a subtle shadow on scroll. Section links glide down the page (they are anchors, not separate pages). Log In currently points to the existing circularexpressbox.com platform so nothing breaks for current users. On phones the links collapse into a hamburger menu.

## 3. Hero

**What it shows:**
Headline "A recycling program in a box." over the supporting line about filling it with bottles, cans, paper, and more, shipping free with FedEx, tracking every pound, and building custom streams for other materials. Beside it: the real studio photo of a hand dropping a bottle into an actual CEB box, tagged "Real box. Real recycling."

**What it does:**
Defines the category in six words and answers the three anxieties every visitor has: is this real (real photo, real logo), is it hard (fill, ship, done), and what do I get (tracking and rewards). Two buttons split the two audiences: consumers go to Pricing, organizations go to For Brands.

**How it works:**
The three chips under the buttons (Prepaid FedEx label included / Live impact dashboard / Points on every shipment) are the compressed sales pitch, each one is expanded by a later section. The eyebrow line "A Geared for GREEN company" borrows four decades of credibility immediately.

## 4. Trusted-by strip

**What it shows:**
Five real names: Miami Waterkeeper, Plastics Industry Association, Padel X Miami, The Content Gym, Coffee and Chill, plus the line "Backed by Geared for GREEN: 40+ years in circular economy, 1.7B+ lbs recycled."

**What it does:**
Social proof in the first scroll. Every name is a genuine relationship, no invented logos, which matters if a prospect ever checks.

**How it works:**
Styled text badges rather than logo images, so no third-party logo permissions are needed and the row never looks pixelated.

## 5. How It Works

**What it shows:**
Header "If you can ship a return, you can run a recycling program," then four numbered cards: Get your box → Fill it up → Ship it free → Earn and track.

**What it does:**
Kills the perceived-effort objection. The return-shipment comparison anchors CEB against a task everyone already knows is easy. Each card carries a friction-killer badge ("No sorting stress," "Prepaid FedEx label").

**How it works:**
This is the actual product flow: order online, boxes arrive flat, material stickers keep streams clean, the prepaid FedEx label prints from the customer's account, and the box is dropped at any FedEx location or handed to a regular driver. Points and dashboard data post when the shipment is received.

## 6. What Goes In

**What it shows:**
Three cards: **Accepted today** (plastic bottles & containers, aluminum cans, paper & cardboard, plus "Other recyclables? Just ask, we can take on more"), **Custom streams** (vinyl records, event & venue waste, textiles and more by request, with a "New: vinyl records" chip), and **Keep it out** (food waste, glass, batteries, hazardous materials).

**What it does:**
Removes the #1 confusion in mail-in recycling: "can I send this?" The explicit not-accepted list protects processing quality and manages expectations honestly. The custom-streams card is the doorway for partner programs, vinyl is listed first on purpose.

**How it works:**
Mirrors the real operating model: each box collects one clean material stream identified by its commodity sticker, which keeps loads uncontaminated and the recovered material valuable.

## 7. Where It Goes

**What it shows:**
A dark-green band tracing the journey: Your box → Regional recycling hub → Sustainable raw material → New products, with the highlight card "6 bottles = 1 hat." and the Geared for GREEN numbers (1.7B+ lbs recycled, 950M+ lbs reintegrated, since 1985).

**What it does:**
Answers the deepest trust question in recycling: "does my stuff actually get recycled?" CEB's unfair advantage over every competitor is that the loop is visible, materials come back as real products, like Champions for GREEN hats stamped with their bottle count. This section is that argument in picture form.

**How it works:**
Boxes ship to regional hubs and material recovery facilities; processed material feeds the G4G family's product lines (apparel, furniture, building products). Every claim in this section comes from company materials.

## 8. Rewards

**What it shows:**
"Recycle to get the rewards." with three example reward tickets: 400 pts for 20% off the 6-Bottle GREEN Hat, 350 pts for $10 off the Peaks Polo, 250 pts for 15% off the Performance Long Sleeve.

**What it does:**
Differentiates from Trashie-style rewards hype. The framing is honest: points buy discounts on gear made from the same kinds of materials you ship. No inflated cash promises, which is exactly where competitors take public criticism.

**How it works:**
Points are credited per received shipment and redeemed on the rewards platform. The three tickets shown are the real redemption offers from the current program.

## 9. Dashboard — the tabbed window

**What it shows:**
Under "Impact you can put in a report.", a mock browser window (with the URL pill "dashboard.circularexpressbox.com") holding two tabs:

  - **Miami Waterkeeper · Beach Cleanups**: the full-season sustainability report, produced in Canva from the team's real report template, in blue with the CEB badge, showing 307 lbs diverted, 480+ volunteers, the material breakdown (bottles 187 lbs, cans 84 lbs, film 21 lbs, foam & cups 9 lbs, caps 6 lbs), three real photos of CEB boxes at actual cleanups, and what the material becomes (4 CIRQBOARD sheets, 7 chairs, 3 benches, 2,041 trash bags).
  - **Gym & Studio Program**: a facility persona in CEB greens, 118 lbs across six months, six boxes, and a rewards card showing 1,180 points redeemed for team gear.

**What it does:**
Shows, not tells, the tracking story: every box becomes numbers a client can put in an ESG report, a member email, or a press release. For a brand prospect, this is the "what do we get" answer. The two tabs cover the two program shapes: community collection events and always-on facility programs.

**What's real and what's illustrative:**
The photos are all real CEB collections. The report design is the team's real Canva template. The program shapes are real (boxes have gone to Miami Waterkeeper cleanups). The specific figures are representative examples at realistic cleanup scale, internally consistent (weights per item, CO2e per material) so they hold up to scrutiny, but they are illustrative rather than audited totals. The page caption says "Example program dashboards." If exact audited numbers become available, they can be swapped in by re-exporting the Canva file.

**How it works:**
Clicking a tab switches panes instantly (no page reload). The Waterkeeper report is a single optimized image exported from the Canva design "CEB Beach Cleanup Sustainability Dashboard - Miami Waterkeeper," which lives in the team Canva account and can be edited there any time; the gym pane is built directly in the page.

## 11. For Brands & Organizations

**What it shows:**
A dark-green band with the real studio photo of a vinyl record being dropped into a CEB box. Headline "Launch a branded take-back program." The checklist: co-branded boxes with your logo on the sponsor panel, custom material streams from vinyl records to event waste, nationwide collection through FedEx, and campaign-level impact reporting for ESG and PR. Plus the line: "Currently piloting record take-back with the music industry."

**What it does:**
This is the Warner Music section. It sells the take-back program as a marketing and ESG asset (the TerraCycle playbook: brands buy engagement and story, not waste management). The vinyl photo plus the pilot line makes a label executive feel the program already exists for them.

**How it works:**
Every physical CEB box carries a "Supported by" sponsor panel and a commodity sticker slot, which is what makes co-branded national programs possible without new infrastructure: branded boxes go anywhere FedEx ships, CEB handles collection, processing, and the impact report. The button opens an email to Jeff Feldman with the subject pre-filled.

An accordion: clicking a question expands its answer and closes the others. Keyboard and screen-reader accessible.

## Under the hood

**Technology:**
The whole site is three hand-built files, `index.html`, `css/styles.css`, `js/main.js`, plus an assets folder. No WordPress, no frameworks, no build step, no plugins to maintain. That makes it fast, secure, and trivially portable: it can be dropped into any host, or wired into the existing site, as-is.

**Where it lives:**

  - **Repository:** [github.com/Jmgordon113/ceb-landing](https://github.com/Jmgordon113/ceb-landing), the handoff point for Katherine. Also contains `SPEC.md`, the full design specification the site was built against, and this overview.
  - **Live preview:** [jmgordon113.github.io/ceb-landing](https://jmgordon113.github.io/ceb-landing/), served by GitHub Pages. Every push to the repo redeploys automatically in about a minute.

**Design system:**
Brand green `#0A572A` sampled directly from the CEB logo, with cream and kraft-cardboard tones echoing the box itself. Headlines in Bricolage Grotesque, body in Inter. Photography policy: only real photos of real boxes, never stock or AI imagery.

**Intentionally deferred:**

  - **Checkout and accounts:** buy buttons and Log In route to the current working platform; a rebuilt checkout is a later phase.
  - **Domain:** circularexpressbox.com still serves the old site until Katherine integrates the new page or the domain is repointed.
  - **Additional pages:** this is deliberately one page; a dedicated records/brands page can be added when a partner program launches.
